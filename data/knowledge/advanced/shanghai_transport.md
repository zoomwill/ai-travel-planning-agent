# Shanghai transport planning

## Bund Pudong and river crossings

The Bund and Pudong can be planned as one geographic pair, but the crossing method should match the traveler's schedule and comfort. Metro routes are predictable for many journeys, while a ferry or other river option can be treated as a scenic choice only after checking current service. Avoid timing a fixed reservation immediately after a discretionary crossing. Photograph skyline views from safe public areas without blocking pedestrian flow. Preserve a land-based backup route in the itinerary in case weather or operations change.

## Metro grouping and transfer buffers

Group activities by Metro corridor and minimize unnecessary changes at large interchange stations. Write station and exit information into the daily notes, but mark it for current verification because exits can close temporarily. A route that looks short on a map may include a long underground walk, so add transfer buffers for luggage, children, or mobility needs. During busy commuting periods, move flexible activities later rather than forcing a tight sequence. Keep the final activity close to a simple route back to the hotel.

## Walking weather and indoor alternatives

Shanghai walking plans should include weather-aware alternatives because heat, rain, wind, and air conditions change the comfortable pace. Pair an outdoor waterfront or lane walk with one nearby indoor option rather than a distant replacement. Carrying photography equipment or shopping bags also reduces practical walking range. The planner should label the outdoor element as flexible and protect only confirmed reservations. Current forecasts and transport notices are external facts and must not be inferred from this static knowledge fixture.
