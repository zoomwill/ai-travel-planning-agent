# Budget-aware travel planning

## Protect the fixed-cost buffer

A realistic budget starts by separating transport and lodging from flexible daily spending. Do not allocate the entire stated budget: keep a visible contingency for local transit, baggage, taxes, meals, and price changes. Compare options using the same currency and traveler count, and never treat a cached or mock price as a booking quote. When the plan is over budget, first reduce room category, optional paid attractions, or long transfers before removing every preference-led activity. Show assumptions so the traveler can replace them.

## Free and paid activity balance

Balance one high-priority paid experience with parks, neighborhood walks, public architecture, viewpoints, or markets that do not require an admission promise. Free activities still have transport, meal, and time costs, so they are not literally costless. Rank them by the traveler's interests and geographic fit rather than by price alone. Keep an indoor low-cost fallback for poor weather. Confirm current admission fees independently, since static local knowledge can guide the shape of a day but cannot provide a live ticket total.

## Daily tracking and decision points

Give each day a simple spending ceiling and identify which optional item can be skipped if earlier costs run high. A decision point is more useful than a false exact forecast: for example, choose the paid viewpoint only if the weather and remaining budget support it. Record whether meals are estimates per person or for the whole party. Avoid converting currencies with an invented exchange rate. The final plan should say which amounts come from deterministic mock providers and which amounts need the traveler to verify before purchase.
